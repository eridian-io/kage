Option A

Terminal-first

The browser becomes your tmux. Each session is a tab, and the whole screen is a live, fully interactive terminal. You see exactly what you'd see over SSH, and you can type anything.

Good at

  • Nothing is hidden. Every key works, including Claude Code's own menus and shortcuts.
  • Feels like home to anyone who lives in tmux.
  • Least "Kage UI" to design, since the terminal is the UI.

Costs

  • The terminal engine (xterm.js) loads on every visit and streams every byte while a tab is open.
  • To see what needs you, you have to read the terminal yourself.
  • Rough on a phone: small text, and Ctrl and Esc need an extra key row.
Option C · take 1

Board: list on the left, session on the right

This is closest to your whiteboard. Sessions are grouped by what they need from you. The right side shows a read-only live tail, the question Claude is asking as buttons, and the session's ports and changed files. Good at

  • What needs you is clear at a glance: amber at the top, and the question shown as buttons.
  • The page loads small and fast. The full terminal costs nothing until you open it.
  • Everything per session is in one place: preview ports, changed files, Stop.

Costs

  • Kage must recognise Claude's questions to turn them into buttons. Hooks give the "waiting" signal; the options come from the screen text, with a free-text reply as the fallback.
  • Two panes need a wide screen. On a phone this becomes list → detail (see below).

Old sessions

  • Try it: open a Done session and use Pin, Archive or Delete.
  • Pin keeps the sessions worth revisiting at the top. Archive hides the rest but keeps them searchable, and it can happen automatically once the PR is merged.
  • Delete removes the worktree and branch, and warns you first if any work isn't pushed.
Option C · take 2

Inbox: what needs you comes first

Treat sessions like an inbox. Anything waiting for you is a big card you can answer on the spot. Running sessions get one line each with their latest output, and finished ones offer the next step. It's built for a quick check-in, not for watching.

For people who never touch the mouse. One row per session, like htop, with a live preview of the selected one underneath. Everything has a key, and ⌘K opens a palette where you can start a task, jump to a session or open a port.

A vs C

What you gain and give up

The sizes are rough targets for planning, not measurements. C still contains everything A has; it just loads the terminal when you open it.

A · Terminal-firstC · Dashboard + terminal on demand
First load~110 KB gzipped (app + xterm.js)~20 KB gzipped. The terminal adds ~90 KB once, when first opened, then stays cached
Data while watchingEvery byte of every open terminalSmall state events, plus output for the one session on screen
Seeing what needs youRead each tab's outputAmber at the top, with a phone notification
Answering a questionSwitch tab, read, type 1 ⏎One tap on the option, from the notification or the list
Doing anything unusualAlways possiblePossible, one click away (Open terminal)
PhoneUsable in a pinchDesigned for it
Weak connection (train, 3G)Laggy typing; reconnects redraw the screenSmall updates; buttons work even on a slow link
Effort to buildLower UI work, since the terminal is the UIMore UI, plus parsing Claude's questions into buttons
My recommendation

C, built from all three takes

  • Desktop: the C1 board, with the full terminal opening in place of the live tail.
  • Phone: the C2 inbox, so a notification leads to a card and then to a one-tap answer.
  • Everywhere: the C3 keyboard layer (j/k, r, t, ⌘K), so power users never need the mouse.

Questions to settle

  1. A or C? And if C, does the mix above feel right, or does one take win outright?
  2. Watching vs checking in. Do you mostly glance and answer (C2 leans this way), or keep a session open and watch it work (C1 and A lean this way)?
  3. Dark only, or a light theme too? Dark only keeps the first version smaller.
  4. Anything missing from the session view? For example cost or tokens used, run time, or a "what Claude is doing now" summary line.
