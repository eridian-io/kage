Six ways to draw the board

Variant 0 is C1 as it is now. Variants 1–5 keep the same job (pick a session, see its output, answer it, open its preview) but change how loud it is and how it's arranged. The sessions are the same in all of them. Click the sessions in each mock-up to switch between them.

Variant 0 · baseline

Current C1

Your favourite so far, for reference: lime and amber accents, a pulsing "needs you" dot, a black terminal box and an amber question bar.

my-pc:8080

Why it might be too much

Glows, a pulse, a gradient, filled buttons, three columns and two status colours all compete. Everything asks for attention at once.

Variant 1

Quiet

The same layout with the volume turned down. No glows, pulses or boxes; the output sits on the page like text in an editor. Lime marks what's alive and what you can act on (the selected session, New task, the live cursor, preview links), and amber appears only when a session is waiting for you.

my-pc:8080

Feels like

A calm writing app or a minimal code editor.

Changes from current

The same three columns, but drawn with thin lines instead of boxes. The preview pane on the right keeps ports, changed files and the worktree. All actions sit in one footer under the output: Terminal on the left, Stop, Archive and Delete on the right, away from the answer buttons.

Trade-off

Less scannable from across the room. You rely on the list's amber line to spot what needs you.

Variant 2

Daylight

A light theme with soft cards. The output is shown like a code review rather than a raw terminal, with diffs highlighted. It feels more like a polished SaaS tool than a hacker console.

my-pc:8080

Feels like

A modern code-review or project tool in daylight.

Changes from current

Light colours, rounded cards, search in the header, status shown as soft pills.

Trade-off

It breaks from the dark Kage brand. Most likely this becomes the light mode of whichever dark direction you pick, not the main look.

Variant 3

Terminal UI

The whole app looks like a polished terminal program, like lazygit or htop: monospace everywhere, thin framed panels, status as glyphs, and every action shown as a key. There are no buttons, so nothing shouts.

my-pc:8080

Feels like

lazygit or k9s in a browser tab. Developers will feel at home.

Changes from current

All text is monospace, panels have titled frames, and a key bar replaces the buttons. It still works with the mouse.

Trade-off

Dense, and it can feel cold. Also the hardest to adapt to a phone later.

Variant 4

Conversation

Each session reads like a chat with Claude: your task at the top, Claude's messages in a calm centred column, tool calls folded into small grey lines, and the question as the last message with a reply box underneath.

my-pc:8080

Feels like

A chat app or claude.ai, but for sessions running on your own machine.

Changes from current

The session reads top to bottom like a document. Preview, files and terminal move to small buttons in the header.

Trade-off

Kage must turn terminal output into messages, which means more parsing. Raw output is one click away (Terminal).

Variant 5

Wall

Every session is a tile with its own small live output, like a wall of monitors. Click a tile and its details slide in from the right. Close the panel to go back to the overview. Try it: click "fix login bug".

my-pc:8080

Feels like

A CI dashboard or a wall of security-camera feeds: all your agents working at once.

Changes from current

No permanent list. The overview is the main screen, and details are a panel you open and close.

Trade-off

Great for watching many sessions; busier when 10+ tiles are moving. It streams more output (one small feed per tile).

Pick a direction

Which one feels right?

  • Pick one as is, or combine them, for example the layout of 1 with the conversation view of 4, or the overview of 5 as an extra screen.
  • My pick: Variant 1 (Quiet), with the conversation view from 4 as an optional way to read a session. It keeps the C1 layout you like, removes what makes it "in your face", and costs the least to render, which fits the performance goal.
  • Variant 2's light theme can become the light mode of whatever we pick.
